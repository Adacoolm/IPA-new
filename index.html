Executive Summary

Problem:

Nigeria has over 191,000 registered non governmental organizations (NGOs) according to data from the Corporate Affairs Commission. They operate across various fields, yet there are over 133 million people living in poverty in Nigeria alone. While not all have access to advanced technology, roughly 70% have access to a mobile phone.

Why it matters:

The resources are out there, but the connection between those who need them and those who provide them is almost nonexistent.

Proposed Solution:

A simple idea to counter this problem: a platform that matches users to the programs that fit their situation, with AI helping to clarify user confusion.

How it works:

The user provides information about themselves and their current situation. We compare their profile with verified programs and show relevant options with requirements, offerings, and application steps. AI helps users understand their results and answer questions, but does not make final eligibility decisions.

Benefits/Impact:

The platform alleviates poverty by increasing vulnerable populations' access to suitable development and aid programs via mobile devices, reaching community members with phones, who constitute ~70% of the 133 million people living in poverty.

Resources needed:

For the prototype, we utilize free hosting, database, and AI/API tiers. WhatsApp, SMS, and USSD can be added later. Development after launch is estimated at ₦1–2.4 million for two developers over two months, with annual maintenance at ₦150,000–₦600,000.

Expected Outcome:

A sustainable reduction in poverty rates by connecting people to the exact help they need.

CONTEXT

Introduction

The poverty rate in Nigeria stands at approximately 62% to 63% (Vanguard article: read the source). That is roughly 140 million people with poor living standards.

In 2024, Nigeria had 191,278 registered non governmental organizations (NGOs) according to official data from the Corporate Affairs Commission (The Guardian).

In Nigeria and around the world, governments, NGOs, organizations, and individuals set up forms of aid to improve livelihoods. But just because these initiatives exist does not mean they are accessible to the common person.

Many low income families, farmers, and youths are not aware of opportunities that would benefit them. Information is scattered across various platforms, social media, websites, and channels, and specific programs may not be suitable for them.

DAAC aims to bridge the gap between these opportunities and people who need them by understanding an individual’s situation based on the information they provide, matching them with appropriate programs and resources to reduce poverty in the long run.

THE CHALLENGE · CASE STUDY

Problem Statement & Case Study

The Structural Gap

Based on figures, the ratio of NGOs to poor persons in Nigeria is approximately 1:737 (widening further when inactive NGOs are removed). While organizations want to help, are they providing the right kind of help? Many local NGOs underperform because they come in with preplanned agendas and standard interventions without mapping exact community needs. Furthermore, application criteria buried in PDFs act as literacy barriers, while organizations struggle to find the right beneficiaries.

Empirical Research: The MIT/Bangladesh Asset Trap Study (2022)

Overview: A landmark longitudinal study spanning 11 years by MIT and LSE economists tracked 6,000 households in rural Bangladesh (Source: read the Phys.org source).

Core Finding: Poverty is not the product of a person's capabilities or attitudes; the very poor are trapped because they lack initial access to productive assets or opportunities to clear a basic wealth threshold.

The Intervention & Impact: Providing a one time resource (like a single cow) with training allowed impoverished women to shift from low wage labor to sustainable livestock rearing and land cultivation, permanently escaping the poverty trap.

Takeaway: The poor do not lack drive or ability; they lack baseline structural opportunities. DAAC addresses this exact infrastructural gap.

THE SOLUTION

Proposed AI driven Solution

DAAC helps people find support programmes they qualify for across agriculture, training, employment, finance, business, and social support.

Users provide their location, age, occupation, income range, household size, and business/farm details. The system compares this profile with verified programs to show relevant options.

The platform is accessible to users with low literacy or limited access to electricity via USSD codes and voice helplines, with their information logged in our systems. AI looks through the database for matching programs and delivers options via text, allowing users to trigger USSD applications or opt in for future notifications.

AI is used for data collection, speech translation, extraction, and matching, but never makes final eligibility decisions (which are set by each program).

It is built as a system that sustains itself through long term training in farming, vocations, and education rather than short term handouts.

“Give a man a fish and you feed him for a day; teach a man to fish and you feed him for a lifetime.”

GROWTH & OPERATIONS

Scalability & Sustainability

Built to scale

Scalability is evaluated based on the prototype's ability to grow and its cost efficiency. The system will initially be piloted in a targeted community before expanding to additional states and countries.

Works with existing systems

Integrates with existing organizational tools rather than replacing them. AI creates beneficiary profiles and flags barriers, while staff make final decisions.

Sustained through partnership

Sustainability over the long term relies on partnerships with NGOs, government agencies, and research institutions, combined with a tiered model where small organizations get basic tools and larger programs pay for advanced analytics.

OUTCOMES & MEASUREMENT

Expected Impact

Evidence from graduation programmes

The project draws on the Innovations for Poverty Action (IPA) Graduation Approach, which boosts household income by 32–45%, per capita consumption by 18–25%, and productive assets by 40–88%.

Models in practice

This includes models such as the Kaduna State Ultra Poor Graduation Program (KATSIPA), The BOMA Project REAP Model (Kenya), and the National Skills Training Strategy (NPRGS).

Lasting change

DAAC increases vulnerable populations' access to development programs. In the short term, we improve access to initiatives; in the medium term, we provide reliable economic empowerment; in the long term, we reduce poverty rates. We will monitor progress via surveys and organizational data over 3–12 months.

THE WAY FORWARD

Conclusion

The challenge of poverty eradication in Nigeria is not a lack of available resources or humanity; rather, it is a crisis of connection and accessibility.

While thousands of life changing programs exist, they remain hidden behind walls, fragmented platforms, and complex application processes that exclude the very people who need them most.

DAAC bridges this divide by blending human centered design, multiple platform methods like USSD and voice for offline communities, and targeted AI matching. This transforms how aid is delivered.

We are moving away from temporary empowerment and building a bridge that sustains itself toward permanent economic independence for the poor.

In conclusion, DAAC ensures that opportunity finally meets the people who are ready to use it.
