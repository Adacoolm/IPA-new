PROJECT PROPOSAL

Introduction

The poverty rate in Nigeria stands at approximately 62% to 63% (Vanguard). That is roughly over 140 million people with poor living standard.

In 2024, Nigeria had 191,278 registered non-governmental organizations (NGOs) according to official data from the Corporate Affairs Commission (The Guardian).

Beyond Nigeria and all over the world, governments, NGOs, organizations and individuals set up forms of aid in order to improve the livelihoods of those in need. But just because these much initiatives exist does not mean that they are accessible to the common person.

Many low income families, farmers and youths are not aware of the opportunities that would benefit them and aid their current situation. Also information is scattered across various platforms, different social media, websites and channels and the specific programs people do know of may not be suitable for them.

DAAC aims to solve the gap between these opportunities and people who need then by understanding an individual’s situation and need, based on information they release about themselves, and match them with appropriate programs and resources reducing poverty rate in the long run.

THE CHALLENGE

PROBLEM STATEMENT

Based on the figures provided, the ratio of NGO to poor person on Nigeria is approximately 1:737. Removing NGOs that are inactive or just on 'paper', the ratio widens even more. While these organizations may be able to help people, are they giving the right kind of help to those who need it?

Many local NGOs underperform because they come in with "pre-planned agendas". They roll out standard interventions without first mapping the exact demographic profile, needs, or pre-existing knowledge of the community. Therefore they can't tailor their services to fit the community they're trying to help.

Development and upskilling programs have different eligibility requirements, application processes and their information is mostly transferred and widespread. For people with low literacy, this could be a barrier to navigating these systems and applying to these opportunities especially when criteria and knowledge is buried beneath PDFs.

On the side of organizations, while social media exists and physical campaigns can be done, finding the right individuals who can properly benefit from their programs may be difficult.

This brings the problem: resources are out there, but the connection between those who need it and those who provide them is almost nonexistent.

THE SOLUTION

AI DRIVEN SOLUTION

DAAC is a platform that helps people find support programmes they may qualify for. Nigeria already has programmes for agriculture, training, employment, finance, business and social support, but the information is often scattered across different organisations and platforms. For someone looking for help, knowing what is available to them can be difficult.

The idea is simple: tell us a little about your situation, and we help you find the programmes that may fit. A user could provide their state or LGA, age, occupation, income range, household size and, where relevant, information about their farm or business. We then compare that profile with information from verified programmes and show the user relevant options, including their requirements, what they offer and how to apply.

This process, for those who don't have access to constant light/electricity can be donw with the use of a USSD code/phone number for those that can't write. The code is typed in and then the user is prompted to write about their location, needs/situation and other details. Or in the case of the helpline, the user is prompted to select their language and then speak. This information provided would be logged into our systems.

The AI is then used to look through our database of registered programmes for those that they qualify for and closely suit the person's needs and deliver it to them via text. The option would be given to them to send a USSD code based on the program they're most interested in.

There would also be the choice to opt-in for continual notifications should opportunities arise in the future.

For the more digital literate, a website would be available for ease of access.

If the platform has widespread use, it can act as a data collection avenue for these organizations and support programmes that can be used to gain better information on demographic needs.

Artificial Intelligence is part of the platform in data collection, speech translation, extraction and programmes matching. But it is not making the final eligibility decision. The matching itself will use the requirements set by each programme.

We're not planning on building a short-term relief solution by providing access to only handout packages, food aid and distribution, We plan on building a self-sustaining system where individuals can be matched up to programmes that will train them in different areas such as Farming and Agriculture, Trading and Vocations, Education and so much more.

After all "Give a man a fish, and you feed him for a day; teach a man to fish, and you feed him for a lifetime."

LONG-TERM GROWTH

SCALABILITY & SUSTAINABILITY

For scalability we're looking at two deciding factors: Can this work beyond the prototype? and can it keep operating without becoming too expensive and dependent on us or the development team.

The system would initially be piloted with a small number programs and designated to targeted communities. Once this run is validated, feedback collected and improvements made, it can then be expanded to additional programs, states, and eventually other countries facing similar challenges.

One thing to note is that the system does not require organizations to replace their existing processes, rather it is a way for them to integrate the tools they already use for their programs into our platform in order to improve beneficiary-to-program matching, identify gaps in outreach, and efficiently target available resources.

In the area of beneficiary-to-program matching, AI would be used to create beneficiary profiles, identify patterns, flag possible barriers, and verify evidence. The programme staff/organization themselves make the final decisions.

Long-term sustainability would come through partnerships with NGOs, government agencies, development organizations, and research institutions, plus a model where small organizations have access to basic monitoring tools and larger programs pay for advanced analytics and reporting.

PROGRAMME EVIDENCE

EXPECTED IMPACT

Before looking at the expected impact of the proposed solution, we must first look at the impact of education/support programmes in poverty alleviation.

The Innovations for Poverty Action's Graduation Approach boosts household income by 32–45% and per capita consumption by 18–25% (as seen in evaluations across settings like Uganda's refugee settlements)

Generates a 40–88% increase in productive assets like livestock and land.

and drastically increases savings and borrowing behaviors, with savings multiplying significantly relative to comparison or near-poor groups.

Other programmes like this include the:

The Kaduna State Ultra-Poor Graduation Program

Fully led by a state government, this is the first ultra-poor graduation initiative of its kind in West Africa. Coordinated through the Kaduna State Social Investment Program Agency (KATSIPA), it targets the state's most vulnerable households on the social register earning less than $3 per day.

The BOMA Project REAP Model (Kenya)

The Rural Entrepreneur Assessment Project (REAP) is a prominent, gender-focused poverty graduation model evaluated by researchers alongside IPA Kenya.

National Skills Training Strategy: A coordinated sub-program managed under the National Poverty Reduction with Growth Strategy (NPRGS) targets 5 million young Nigerians, offering intense vocational, entrepreneurship, and digital empowerment packages to ensure market-driven graduation from extreme poverty among others.

Programmes we didn't know about if not for our research. Part of the impact of these programmes falls back to us if people are connect through our platform.

MEASUREMENT

EXPECTED IMPACT

The DAAC platform is expected to alleviate poverty by increasing the accessibility of the vulnerable to suitable development and aid programmes. As a phone application, the platform will impact vulnerable community members with phones (constituting about 70% of the 133 million Nigerians living in poverty). In the short term, we aim to improve accessibility to poverty alleviation initiatives. In the medium term, we intend for the platform to serve as a reliable means for vulnerable populations to receive education, economic empowerment, and aid. In the long term, we expect DAAC to facilitate sustainable change by reducing poverty rates.

We intend to monitor and measure progress through survey forms, follow-up and feedback and data from programmes and organizations with impact over 3 - 12 months.

IMPLEMENTATION

BUDGET AND PROJECT TIMELINE

The pilot version can be a working prototype of a website built without a large upfront budget using free hosting, database, and AI/API tiers.

This would be tested in a local environment to gain feedback and add improvements. WhatsApp, SMS and USSD can be added later on as the project scales. The downside is that the pilot version may not be scalable in very rural areas with low digital literacy in a website form.

This would cost about 300 thousand Naira

After the hackathon, we estimate ₦1–2.4 million for two developers over roughly two months, with technical maintenance estimated at ₦150,000–₦600,000 per year.
